## 1. Character Lore & Narrative Universe

### 1.1 The Premise: "Countdown to Ares-1"
It is the year 2038. Humanity stands on the precipice of its greatest achievement: the launch of **Ares-1**, the first crewed interplanetary transport vessel carrying 12 pioneering astronauts to Mars.

However, a human landing fleet cannot survive on an unconditioned alien world. Three months prior to Ares-1's arrival, Earth Command deployed an automated Habitat Bio-Dome and a solo advance engineer to Elysium Planitia / Jezero Basin:

- **Protagonist: Cadet Leo (Age 12)**  
  A student astronaut prodigy selected through the global NASA Youth Pioneer Program. Leo is resourceful, curious, and skilled in mechanical diagnostics and power routing.
- **Companion: Chipi-Bot (Mk-IV Outpost Drone)**  
  A compact, hovering bipedal drone equipped with an acoustic sensor array and NASA historical database. Chipi-Bot delivers telemetry warnings with humorous, reassuring dialogue (< 15 words) and provides hints when Cadet Leo faces complex scientific dilemmas.
- **Mission Goal:**  
  Over the course of **20 Martian Sols**, Cadet Leo must test the outpost’s life support, collect baseline planetary specimens, survive environmental crises (frigid night, dust storms, hull fractures), and activate the high-gain sky laser beacon to welcome Ares-1 safely home.

## 2. Episodic 5-Sol Narrative Scripts

### Sol 1: First Light (Establish Energy Grid & Wipe Regolith Dust)
- **Setting:** Elysium Planitia, twilight transition ($-20^\circ\text{C} \to -60^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Prologue Dialogue:**

  - *Chipi-Bot:* "Dust deposits are cutting solar panel output! Walk 8 meters to the Solar Array, wipe the panels, and return to base."
  - *Mission System:* "Clean the solar panels, collect 2x regolith samples, and return to the Habitat Airlock before night freeze."
- **4-Phase Progression:**
  1. *Briefing:* Leo verifies suit battery in the Bio-Dome.
  2. *Sortie:* Leo walks $8\text{m}$ West to the Solar Array, wipes dust, and collects 2 regolith nodules.

  4. *Lab:* Leo analyzes regolith in the console. The dome's perimeter lights power on.

## 4. Narrative & Pedagogical Counter-Arguments (Phần phản biện)

### Debate 1: Why a solitary child protagonist instead of a full adult astronaut crew?
- **Critique:** *"A 12-year-old managing a Mars base alone is completely unrealistic."*
- **Defense:**
  1. *Psychological Empathy:* Young players (ages 8–15) identify strongly with a peer protagonist ("Cadet Leo"). Adult crews introduce complex interpersonal dynamics that distract from STEM principles.
  2. *Direct Agency:* In a solitary advance scout scenario, every decision rests entirely in the player's hands. There is no automated adult NPC to fix mistakes, fostering genuine engineering accountability.

### Debate 2: Why Chipi-Bot instead of a realistic dry radio voice from NASA Houston?
- **Critique:** *"A floating cartoonish AI companion reduces aerospace realism."*
- **Defense:**
  1. *Affective Scaffolding:* Space can feel lonely and terrifying. Chipi-Bot acts as an emotional anchor, translating scary alarms into actionable, bite-sized tasks.
  2. *Adaptive Pedagogical Hints:* When a child fails a quiz, Chipi-Bot explains the answer warmly, eliminating test anxiety and encouraging creative experimentation.
